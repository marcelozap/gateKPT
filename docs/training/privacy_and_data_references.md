# Privacy And Data References

This file turns pasted privacy/data articles into GateKPT training guidance.

Use it for structure, instincts, and source direction. Do not copy claims into public writing without fresh verification.

## Strong Core Question

> What happens after you hit send?

This starts from the user's action and moves outward into the system.

## Data Journey Pattern

The privacy version of the AI layers should follow a practical chain:

1. The user types or uploads something.
2. The message leaves the device.
3. The service processes it on company infrastructure.
4. A model generates a response.
5. The conversation may be stored.
6. Human review may happen.
7. The content may or may not be used for training depending on product, plan, settings, region, and date.
8. Retention rules decide how long data stays.
9. Legal demands, security incidents, shared links, or product features can change the risk.

This is a strong GateKPT pattern: start with the obvious action, then uncover the hidden layers.

## Editorial Lessons From The Pasted Articles

- Start with a familiar moment: typing a question and pressing send.
- Make the invisible system visible.
- Explain tradeoffs instead of fearmongering.
- Separate consumer, business, enterprise, and local-model use cases.
- Treat privacy settings as helpful but incomplete.
- Make plan tier, region, and date explicit when discussing policies.
- End with practical behavior: what not to paste, what settings to check, when to use business or local tools.

## Claims That Require Fresh Verification

Verify these before publishing:

- Whether a platform trains on consumer conversations by default.
- Whether users can opt out, and where the setting lives.
- Whether conversations can be reviewed by employees or contractors.
- Deletion and retention windows.
- Separate retention for reviewed conversations.
- Differences between free, paid consumer, business, enterprise, API, and workspace products.
- Regional differences for the US, EEA, UK, Switzerland, and other jurisdictions.
- Litigation, legal holds, subpoenas, preservation orders, and data handovers.
- Claims about browser screenshots, agent mode, file access, Gmail access, overlays, keystrokes, or device metadata.
- Any breach or leak claim.

## Source Direction

Prefer primary sources:

- OpenAI privacy policy and Data Controls documentation.
- Google Gemini Apps Privacy Hub and Workspace privacy documentation.
- Anthropic privacy center and consumer terms.
- Court filings for litigation claims.
- Company incident reports for breach claims.

Secondary sources may help with framing, but do not use them as the only support for a specific policy claim.

## Possible GateKPT Note

Working title:

`What Happens After You Hit Send`

Possible thesis:

AI does not begin and end in the chat box. The moment you hit send, your words enter a chain of product decisions and physical systems: processing, context, storage, review, training, retention, legal exposure, chips, cooling, and power.

The point is not to scare people away from AI. The point is to make the invisible system visible enough that people can choose how to use it.
