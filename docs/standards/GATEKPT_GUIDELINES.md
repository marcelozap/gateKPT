# GateKPT Guidelines

**The standard for every post, page, and line of copy in this project.**
Companion to `PROJECT_GUIDELINES.md`, which covers the codebase, design system, and architecture. This file governs what GateKPT says.

## Core idea

GateKPT is not AI hype. It is a public way to understand AI from the ground up: power, chips, data, models, software, testing, and business.

Three claims, in order:

1. AI is not just a chatbot.
2. AI is infrastructure.
3. AI is a new way work gets done.

## Voice

Write like a builder, not a guru.

**Is:** direct, curious, slightly rebellious, practical, human, fearless, anti-excuse, pro-learning

**Is not:** corporate AI marketing, crypto hype, “AI will replace everyone,” fake motivation, or technical language used for its own sake

## What GateKPT stands for

- Learning in public
- Breaking down complex systems
- Helping non-engineers understand technical change
- Helping business people stop hiding behind “I’m not technical”
- Showing that AI depends on real layers: energy, hardware, data, software, trust, and adoption
- Turning fear into movement

## Content pillars

**1. AI is physical.** Electricity, chips, data centers, cooling, cable, and land.

**2. AI is work.** It changes how people write, code, design, analyze, manage, and decide.

**3. AI is a bridge.** Managers do not need to become engineers. They need to understand how the system works.

**4. AI requires trust.** If you cannot test it, measure it, or explain it, you cannot trust it.

**5. AI rewards action.** The future belongs to people willing to try, break things, rebuild, and keep moving.

## Posting rules

Every post does at least one of these:

- Teach one thing clearly
- Challenge an excuse
- Explain one layer
- Connect AI to real work
- Push someone toward action
- Make a technical idea feel reachable

Mechanics that hold across every post:

- The first line is the whole post. LinkedIn truncates around 140–210 characters. If the hook is not in the first two lines, it does not exist.
- Two to three sentences. Less is more.
- The image carries the setup. Never write out what the picture already says.
- Never ask two questions. One gets answered; two get skipped.
- Layer names in a post must match the ordered layer definitions in `src/gatekpt/content.ts` exactly.
- English and Spanish are part of the public standard. Keep both versions honest and in sync where a translation exists.

## Tone examples

**Good**

> AI is not magic. It is power, chips, data, software, testing, and business decisions stacked together.

> “I’m not technical” is not a personality trait. It is a skill gap. Skill gaps can be closed.

> You do not need permission to learn the future.

> Worst case you end up back where you started. You are already there.

**Avoid**

> AI is the most revolutionary paradigm-shifting innovation of our lifetime.

> Everyone who does not use AI will be obsolete.

Insulting the people you are trying to reach guarantees they stop reading, and neutral readers side with whoever got attacked. Aim at the condition, never the person.

## Words

| Reach for | Avoid |
|---|---|
| map, layer, stack, anchor, constraint | journey, unlock, empower, supercharge |
| what breaks, failure mode, bottleneck | pain point, challenge |
| context, structure, economics | game-changing, revolutionary, 10x |
| hold, carry, traverse | dive in, explore, get started |
| observed, measured, sourced | proven, guaranteed, best-in-class |

Public-safety substitutions for market-adjacent content:

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| trading | market context |
| signals | indicators |
| alpha | insight |
| plays | scenarios |
| price targets | valuation context |
| buy/sell | coverage |

## Sentence rules

- Keep sentences under 25 words when practical.
- Use plain words. If a normal person would not use it, do not.
- Prefer nouns over adjectives. “Grid interconnect queue” beats “cutting-edge infrastructure.”
- Use numbers over intensifiers. “Cost fell 4.1x” beats “cost fell dramatically.”
- Every claim with a number gets a source.
- No exclamation points.
- No rhetorical questions as headings.
- State the fact and stop.

## Visual direction

Use images that show movement, leaving comfort zones, old systems against new, maps and layers and terminals and field logs, human curiosity, physical infrastructure, adventure, and risk.

On borrowed characters, referencing a film’s feeling is fine. Reproducing a studio’s character art on a professional profile is a risk worth avoiding once the account matters. Prefer original imagery that carries the same idea without copying a character design.

Verify plot details before publishing. Getting a movie beat wrong costs more credibility than the reference was worth.
